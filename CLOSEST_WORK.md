# Closest-work audit -- Phase 1

**Search freeze:** 2026-10-05 (Asia/Bangkok). Search methods and candidate
decisions are in `literature/search_log.csv` and `literature/candidate_library.csv`.
This is a novelty audit, not an outcome synthesis.

## Narrow, defensible contribution position

The audit does not support an unrestricted priority claim. It supports the
narrower contribution to be tested in this project: a reproducible public-data
audit that jointly compares blind dream expression, BLV-originating information
intent, and human/AI accessibility texts with one pre-specified sensory,
spatial, social, safety, uncertainty, and agency ontology. The resulting
comparison is descriptive; it does not establish cognitive transfer or dream
change caused by AI.

## Closest novelty threats

### Kang et al. (2023): blind dream sensory analysis

**Did/data/found:** Analyzed 180 DreamBank reports from seven congenitally blind
people against 981 sighted-control reports using sensory keywords; reported more
auditory, haptic, gustatory/olfactory wording and less color/aesthetic wording.

**Did not do:** It did not analyze BLV waking questions, modern AI descriptions,
human-verified guidance, social-impact dimensions, or cross-domain alignment.

**Our added value:** A wider frozen ontology and a comparison across released
dream, BLV-intent, human-description, and AI-guidance text while retaining the
critical distinction between expression in a report and experienced sensation.

### Ilic et al. (2023): dream/visuo-spatial systematic review

**Did/data/found:** Synthesized dream imagery and sensory-substitution evidence
for congenital/early blindness.

**Did not do:** No primary corpus alignment with BLV accessibility information or
AI outputs.

**Our added value:** Translates the review's conceptual boundary into a
reproducible HCI audit without treating visual imagery as the only relevant
sensory construct.

### Meaidi et al. (2014): controlled dream sensory construction study

**Did/data/found:** Collected four weeks of prompted dream questionnaires from
11 congenitally blind, 14 late-blind, and 25 sighted participants; it reports
on sensory, emotional, thematic, and nightmare outcomes.

**Did not do:** No AI or contemporary accessibility evidence; its participant
prevalence measures cannot substitute for per-dream corpus frequencies.

**Our added value:** Preserves estimands, uses it as literature evidence, and
compares AI-era information distributions with—not as causes of—dream patterns.

### Kreiss et al. (2022) and Mohanbabu & Pavel (2024): BLV description context

**Did/data/found:** These studies show that context matters for accessible image
descriptions and report BLV preference for context-aware descriptions.

**Did not do:** Neither includes blind dream phenomenology or a shared audit of
BLV questions, human descriptions, AI outputs, and verified guidance.

**Our added value:** Makes BLV-defined intent a comparison distribution and adds
spatial, safety, uncertainty, social-inference, privacy, and agency dimensions.

### Kapur & Kreiss (2024): BLV preference metric bias

**Did/data/found:** Shows reference-based metrics correlate more strongly with
sighted than BLV ratings, motivating user-group-sensitive evaluation.

**Did not do:** Does not analyze AI sensory exposure or dream evidence.

**Our added value:** Tests text-level information alignment directly rather than
using generic reference similarity as its accessibility criterion.

### Xie et al. (2025): Be My AI lived use

**Did/data/found:** Interviews 14 visually impaired Be My AI users and analyzes
descriptions, identifying context, social/identity, and intent limitations.

**Did not do:** Does not link findings to blind dream evidence or a shared
cross-domain quantitative ontology.

**Our added value:** Uses released, license-verified material only and evaluates
which sensory/functional information appears across domains without claiming AI
causes changes in cognition or dreams.

### GuideDog (2026): AI and human-verified guidance corpus

**Did/data/found:** Releases an accessibility-aware guidance resource containing
approximately 22K image-description pairs, about 2K human-verified, and a
depth/object benchmark.

**Did not do:** No blind dream or BLV question layer.

**Our added value:** Uses its human/AI distinction for one component of an
alignment audit whose anchor remains BLV-defined intent and documented dream
evidence.

## Gate decision

**PASS, with refined claim.** Strong closest works occupy each individual layer,
so the submission must not claim that it is the first study of blind dreams,
BLV preferences, or AI accessibility. The proposed cross-domain audit remains
non-trivial only if Phases 2--13 can deliver licensed corpora, reliability-aware
coding, and result-level social implications.
