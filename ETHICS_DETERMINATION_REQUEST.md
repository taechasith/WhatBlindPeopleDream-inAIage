# Request for institutional ethics determination: Study 1

**Study:** Controlled AI-mediated dream incorporation in blind and low-vision
adults
**Protocol:** BD-AI-CHI27-Study1-v2.0
**Status:** draft for submission. `ethics/ETHICS_STATUS.md` is
`NOT_SUBMITTED`; no recruitment, contact, screening, consent, exposure,
recording, or data collection has begun.

## Activity requiring determination

This is prospective human-participant research. Adults who identify as blind or
low-vision would complete four home-based pre-sleep sessions, spaced 6--8 days
apart. Each session uses one accessible, non-sensitive prototype scene or
neutral narrative through a common TTS route within a fixed session envelope:

1. Default-AI scene description (DAI).
2. Participant-steered-AI scene interaction (SAI).
3. Matched human-authored reference description (HUM).
4. Active neutral, non-scene audio narrative (ANC).

The current balance design contains up to 208 anonymous allocation slots. It is
not a roster or evidence of enrollment. The final recruitment target and any
change to this maximum require completion of the precollection power check,
institutional review, and preregistration consistency.

After sleep, participants may voluntarily provide a neutral, free dream report
in accessible text or audio. The primary analysis is blinded, target-versus-decoy
coding of the first eligible free report after DAI/SAI sessions. No explicit
semantic or spatial reconstruction task occurs between exposure and sleep. A
delayed task may occur only after the free report if it is ultimately approved,
specified, and consented. The current prospective protocol is
`PROTOCOL_STUDY1_v2.md`; v1.0 is retained only as a historical record.

The project must not be treated as exempt merely because it is accessibility
research or because it does not include neuroimaging. It involves a disability
population, sleep-adjacent procedures, dream narratives that can disclose
sensitive information, and AI-generated content.

## Questions for the responsible institutional ethics office

1. Is this activity human-subjects research under the applicable policy?
2. Does it require full review, expedited review, exemption, or another local
   determination before recruitment and pilot testing?
3. Are home morning reports acceptable under the proposed four-session design,
   and are additional safeguards required for audio/text reports, repeated
   sleep-adjacent sessions, or a post-report task?
4. What accessibility, consent, data-security, adverse-event, compensation, and
   community-engagement conditions are required?
5. What records may be included in an anonymous conference submission without
   revealing investigators or institution?

## Risk controls proposed for review

- Screen and license all stimuli; exclude identifying, traumatic, sexual,
  medical, graphic, and private-location images.
- Make all materials screen-reader compatible; offer accessible consent and the
  ability to pause, skip, withdraw, or delete a session's report where legally
  and technically possible.
- Do not promise dream induction, therapeutic benefit, improved cognition, or
  safe AI accuracy.
- Collect free reports before any target-specific prompt; do not use deception
  about AI use, recording, the number of sessions, or study burden.
- Retain raw dream audio/text and AI transcripts in access-controlled storage;
  do not publish verbatim sensitive reports or source images by default.
- Freeze and audit AI outputs; prohibit unreviewed, live-model variability unless
  the ethics submission explicitly permits and mitigates it.
- Define distress/fatigue procedures, contact paths, adverse-event handling, and
  staff training before recruitment.
- Prepare an advisory-review plan for BLV community input on accessibility,
  burden, and wording. Do not contact or compensate advisers until the
  responsible office confirms that the activity is permitted or the applicable
  determination/approval is in place. Any substantive contribution may warrant
  authorship review rather than automatic acknowledgement.
